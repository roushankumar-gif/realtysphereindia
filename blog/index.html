How to Evaluate a Residential Project Before Buying

A clear, practical checklist to help you compare location, approvals, total costs and project information before shortlisting a home.

REALTYSPHERE EDITORIAL TEAM   •   5 MIN READ   •   UPDATED OCTOBER 14, 2026

Quick answer: Evaluate a residential project by checking its location, relevant approvals and RERA records where applicable, total purchase costs, developer information, construction progress, unit specifications and sale agreement. Verify important claims through official sources before paying or signing.

Review the location and daily connectivity

Consider commute routes, public transport, schools, healthcare, shops and other everyday services. Check travel times at the hours you would normally travel. For proposed infrastructure, rely on official announcements and current evidence rather than promotional promises.

Verify applicable RERA registration and approvals

Where registration is required, search the relevant state or union-territory RERA authority using the project name or registration number. Compare the official record with the information provided by the seller. Review the promoter details, declared completion timeline and available project documents. Requirements vary by location and project type.

Buyer note: A brochure or verbal assurance is not a substitute for checking the relevant official records and legal documents.

Understand the full purchase cost

Request a written cost sheet. Depending on the property and jurisdiction, costs beyond the advertised base price may include parking, maintenance deposits, amenity charges, applicable taxes, stamp duty and registration. Ask which items apply, when they are due and whether the amounts can change under the agreement.

Research the developer and construction progress

Review the developer's official information, completed work and relevant public records. If construction is underway, visit the site when practical and compare what you observe with recent official project updates. Read the agreement for payment milestones, possession terms and provisions relating to delays.

Compare the actual home specifications

Check carpet area, floor plan, natural light, ventilation, storage, privacy, accessibility, common areas and the specifications included in the agreement. If you visit a sample unit, confirm whether its finishes and fittings are part of the contracted offering.

Review documents before making a commitment

Ask for relevant title and approval documents, sanctioned plans, allotment terms and the draft agreement. Consider an independent property lawyer's review before signing or making a substantial payment. Keep copies of receipts and written commitments.

Questions to ask before shortlisting

  • What is the applicable RERA registration number, and can I verify it independently?
  • What is included in the quoted price, and what costs are additional?
  • What possession timeline and payment schedule are documented?
  • Which amenities and specifications are included in the agreement?
  • What are the cancellation, refund and delay terms?
  • Which documents should an independent lawyer review?

Final thoughts

A careful property decision combines location research, document verification, realistic budgeting and a review of the home itself. Compare multiple properties using the same checklist, verify key claims with official sources and seek qualified legal or financial advice when needed.
