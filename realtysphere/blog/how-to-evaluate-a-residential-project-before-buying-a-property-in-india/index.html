How to Evaluate a Residential Project Before Buying a Property in India

Short answer: how do you evaluate a residential project?

Check five things before paying any booking amount: the location and how you will actually get to work, school and hospitals; the project’s RERA registration on the official state website; the total cost, not just the base price; the developer’s record on earlier projects; and construction progress against what was promised. Have a lawyer read the agreement before you sign.

1. How do you research location and connectivity?

Visit the area at the times you would travel: weekday mornings and evenings, not a quiet Sunday. Note the roads you would use, public transport you would rely on, and the distance to daily needs.

  • Test the real commute yourself instead of relying on distances quoted in brochures.
  • Check what is around the project: schools, hospitals, markets and workplaces.
  • Confirm land use and any planned infrastructure through the local development authority. Treat “upcoming” claims as unverified until an official source confirms them.

For example, if you are researching Sector 16, Noida, confirm the land use of the specific plot and the approvals for that project before assuming it is residential.

2. Why verify RERA registration?

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA) requires covered projects to be registered with the state authority, which publishes project details such as the promoter, approved plans and declared completion timelines. Registration is a baseline check, not a guarantee of quality.

  1. Open your state RERA website (Uttar Pradesh: UP RERA).
  2. Search by project name or registration number.
  3. Compare promoter name, location, unit types and completion date with what you were told.
  4. Save a copy of the registration page with the date you checked.

If a project says it is registered but you cannot find it, ask for the registration number in writing and check again before paying.

3. What is the total cost of buying a property?

The base price is only one part. Ask for a written cost sheet that lists every item below, and confirm current rates with official sources, as they vary by state and change over time.

Cost itemWhat to ask
Base priceIs it on carpet area, built-up area or super area? RERA uses carpet area.
Stamp duty and registrationCurrent state rates and who pays what. Check the state stamp and registration department.
TaxesApplicable GST (if any) and any tax obligations on your side, such as TDS. Confirm with a tax professional.
Other chargesParking, club membership, maintenance deposit, power backup and similar items.
Home loan costsInterest rate, processing fees and prepayment terms from your lender.

4. How do you research the developer and project progress?

  • List the developer’s earlier projects and check whether they were delivered on the promised timeline.
  • Visit completed projects and speak to residents about quality, maintenance and handover.
  • Search RERA complaint and order records and court listings for the promoter’s name.
  • Compare the construction stage on site with the progress declared on the RERA page.
  • For ready homes, ask for the occupancy or completion certificate.

5. Practical buyer checklist

  • RERA registration checked on the official site, with number and date saved
  • Land title and approvals reviewed by a lawyer
  • Written cost sheet covering every charge
  • Developer’s past project delivery verified
  • Site visited more than once, at different times
  • Draft agreement for sale read in full before signing
  • Payment schedule linked to construction stages
  • Loan sanction and affordability confirmed
